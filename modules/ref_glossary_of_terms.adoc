.Glossary of Terms

[cols="1,1"]
|===
^|*Term* ^|*Definition*

|acid
|a substance that can donate a proton (H^+^), has a Ph < 7 on the pH scale

|actinides
|series of 15 heavy, radioactive metals on the periodic table of elements 89 - 103

|aerovane
|meteorological instrument that combines a wind vane with a propeller anemometer to simultaneously measure both wind speed and direction

|Aitken nuclei
|extremely small particles in the atmosphere, typically 0.001 to 0.1 micrometers in diameter, which can serve as nuclei for condensation of water vapor

|alkaline earth metals
|series of soft, reactive elements with two electrons in their outermost shell

|alpha-emitter
|higher energy, heavier particles with shorter range, more capable of killing or destroying a target

|anion
|negatively charged ion

|aromatic hydrocarbon
|an  organic compound containing hydrogen and carbon atoms, most commonly featuring a stable six-carbon ring structure called a benzene ring

|assay
|investigative procedure to measure a specific substance or compound

|anthropogenic
|caused or produced by humans

|Avogadro's number
|6.022x10^23^

|base
|a substance that can accept a proton (H^+^), has a Ph > 7 on the pH scale

|beta decay
|radioactive decay in which an unstable atomic nucleus transforms by emitting a beta particle, which is either an electron or a positron

|beta-emitter
|less intense, lighter particles with longer range, more capable of harming surrounding environment

|burn-up
|amount of nuclear fuel consumed in a reactor

|Calder’s deposition
|mathematical models for atmospheric diffusion and dry deposition

|Cartesian math
|a system for representing data using coordinates on a plane or in space, commonly through having an x-axis and a y-axis

|cation
|positively charged ion

|CIE/Q
|relevant data points include nucleus, nuclear energy, nuclear fission, nuclear fusion, and nuclear radiation

|causation
|two things that are connected in which one of the variables is the specific cause of the other

|chromatography
|a method of measurement that tracks the properties of a substance by analyzing how it interacts with radiation, using properties like light or the propertoes of ions

|constituents
|protons and neutrons that make up a nucleus

|convective acceleration
|the change in velocity experienced by a fluid particle as it moves from one location to another due to a non-uniform flow field

|correlation
|two things that are connected in which one of the variables depends on the other

|cosmogenic
|produced by cosmic rays

|counts per minute (cpm)
|measurement of the rate of events detected by a radiation monitoring instrument, such as a Geiger counter

|covariance
|a statistical measure that shows how much two variables change together

|crosswind
|wind blowing in a direction not parallel to a course

|crustal
|originated in the earth's crust

|dibenzothiophene
|a tricyclic organic sulfur compound with the formula  C~12~H~8~S

|diffusion
|a specialized fabric used in photography and filmmaking to soften light while controlling its spread

|diffusitivity
|measurement rate at which particules, heat, or fluids spread through a medium

|diluent
|inert liquid added to reduce the concentration of a substance

|dispersion
|the phenomenon where a wave's speed depends on its frequency, causing different frequency components to separate

|diurnal
|daily

|disintegrations per minute (dpm)
|a unit of measurement for radioactivity

|disintegration ratio rate
|the rate of disintegration and the ratio of the decayed portion to the total

|downwind
|something or somewhere positioned in the direction of the wind

|eddy energy
|kinetic energy associated with the swirling, turbulent motions a fluid (e.g., ocean, air)

|effluent
|any radioactive material that is released into the environment as waste discharge

|electrical field strength (E/Q)
|a physical field that surrounds electrically charged particles such as electrons

|electrodeposition
|electrochemical process that uses an electric current to deposit a thin layer of a material, such as metal, onto a substrate

|environ
|surrounding area or conditions that may be affected by radiation or nuclear processes

|first order kinetics
|reaction in which the rate is directly proportional to the concentration of a single reactant

|fission
|the process of splitting a heavy atomic nucleus, such as that of uranium or plutonium, into two or more smaller nuclei, releasing a large amount of energy

|flame ionization detector
|a tool that uses a small hydrogen flame to detect and measure organic compounds in a gas sample

|FP
|f-factor in physics, a conversion between radiation exposure (roengens) to the absorbed dose (rads)

|fractionation
|the process of separating a mixture of isotopes or radionuclides into individual components

|fusion
|the process of combining atomic nuclei to form a heavier nucleus, releasing a massive amount of energy

|Gaussian assumption
|the statistical assumption that a dataset or variable follows a normal (or Gaussian) distribution including having (1) a symmetrical bell curve, (2) an equal mean, median, and mode, and (3) a standard deviation

|General Electric Aitken nucleus counter
|device that measures the concentration of airborne particles, produced by general electric

|Geiger-Müller
|portable instrument used to detect and measure ionizing radiation, also known as "Geiger counter"

|geodesic
|the shortest possible line between two points

|geostrophic
|the balance between the coriolis force and the horizontal pressure force in the atmosphere

|half life (t 1/2)
|time required for a quantity of a substance to reduce to half of its initial value through a process of exponential decay

|heptasulfide
|chemical compound containing seven sulfur atoms in each molecule

|heptoxide
|chemical compound containing seven oxygen atoms in each molecule

|inert
|unmoving, stopped

|ingrowth emitter
|a radioactive isotope that produces additional radioactive materials as it decays

|impactor
|an instrument used to sample and size-segregate airborne particles, including radioactive aerosols

|insoluble
|incapable of being dissolved by a substance

|instrumental neutron activation analysis (INAA)
|a non-destructive nuclear analytical technique used to determine the elemental composition of various materials at very low (trace) levels

|inversion layer
|where a layer of warm air is situated above cooler air in the atmosphere

|ionization
|process where an atom loses or gains electrons, becoming a charged ion, typically as a result of interactions with high-energy nuclear radiation like alpha or beta particles

|IPC filter
|air filter made by IPC Eagle (a brand of Idrobase Group)

|isoplething
|plotting a map which connects points of equal value for a specific variable, specifically atmospheric dispersion in this case

|isotope
|two or more forms of the same element with equal numbers of protons each but varying numbers of electrons

|Ivy Mike test
|the codename for the first-full scale test of a thermonuclear device, part of a series in Operation Ivy

|KeV time
|measurement of time that descibes the neutron fluence (time-integrated flux), measured in moles

|KSCM compounds (KSCM)
|synthesized benzofuran compounds, dpm/kscm = disintegrations per 1000 standard cubic meters (dpm/kscm)

|Lagrangian
|tracks the motion of individual particles, measuring their position, velocity, and acceleration overtime by following them through a fluid

|lanthanides
|series of 15 soft, silvery metals in the periodic table of elements 57-71

|Liquid Metal Breeder Reactor (LMBR)
|nuclear reactor that uses a liquid metal (such as liquid sodium) as the primary coolant and is designed to "breed" more fissile material than it consumes

|low background
|experiments or detectors designed to have an extremely low level of background radiation to detect rare nuclear events

|mass number
|the mass number _A_ of an atom is the total number of protons and neutrons in a nucleus (_^A^element_)

|mass spectrometry (m/z)
|analytical technique used to identify and quantify compounds by measuring the mass-to-charge ratio

|megaton (MT)
|1 million tons

|megawatt day exposure (mwd)
|a unit of energy to measure fuel burn-up

|meson
|subatomic particle (also called "pion") studied for its properties in nuclear matter

|metalloids
|elements that exhibits some metal and some nonmetal properties

|micrometer (μm)
|1 millionth of a meter

|mineral residue
|a geological deposit left behind after weathering or an accumulation of minerals in water

|mole
|measurement of the number of atoms, molecules or ions (1 mole = Avogadro's number, = 6.022x10^23^)

|most probable charge (Zp)
|the most likely proton number of a fission fragment within an isobaric chain in nuclear fission

|nanogram (ng)
|1 billionth of a gram

|naphtha
|volatile, often flammable liquid hydrocarbon mixtures used chiefly as solvents and diluents

|neutron capture
|nuclear reaction where a nucleus absorbs a neutron, resulting in a heavier nucleus or a new element

|neutron flux
|Φ, measurement of intensity of neutron radiation

|nitric acid
|corrosive, poisonous with strong oxidizing properties, made in a laboratory by distilling nitrates and sulfuric acid

|nuclear reactor
|a device that houses and controls a sustained nuclear chain reaction, most commonly through fission, to produce a steady and controllable release of energy

|noble gases
|stable gases which have a full outer shell of electrons

|nuclide
|isotope

|oceanographic
|the physical and biological properties and phenomena of the sea

|oil shale
|a fine-grained sedimentary rock containing kerogen, a precursor to petroleum, which can be converted into synthetic crude oil through heating

|orthogonal
|at a right angle

|Pasquill Stability Class (Pasquill Category)
|a system used to classify atmospheric stability and the amount of turbulent mixing in the atmosphere

|perchclorate (ClO [.overline]#~4~#)
|compounds containing the perchlorate anion

|perrhenate (ReO [.overline]#~4~#)
|compounds containing the perrhenate anion

|phenol
|crystalline solid used to synthesize plastics and other materials

|pilot plant
|small-scale, pre-commercial facility used to test and refine the process for a future scale nuclear plant

|pion
|subatomic particle (also called "meson") studied for its properties in nuclear matter

|plume
|gas or aerosol released into the atmosphere at an approximately steady rate

|polycyclic hydrocarbon
|chemical made of only carbon and hydrogen that occur naturally in coal, crude oil, and gasoline

|port injection
|an engine fuel delivery method where fuel is sprayed into the intake port, just before the intake valve, to mix with air before it enters the combustion chamber

|post-transition metals
|soft metals which have lower melting points and are easier to shape

|pounds per square inch (psi)
|a measurement for the amount of pressure resulting from a force with a magnitude of one pound-force applied to an area of one square inch

|process effluent water
|the liquid waste or byproduct that is discharged from an industrial process or a wastewater treatment plant after pollutants have been removed

|production rate
|number of specific nuclei, particles, or radioactive isotopes created per unit of time and per unit of volume

|proportional counter
|a gas-filled ionization detector in nuclear physics that measures the energy of ionizing radiation

|quinoline
|oily liquid used as a solvent that occurs naturally in coal tar and as a product of burning organic material

|radioactive decay
|the process where an unstable atomic nucleus loses energy by emitting radiation

|radioactive yield
|percentage of a radioactive product's activity compared to the starting radioactivity, both decay-corrected to the same point in time

|radiotoxicity
|percentage of a radioactive product's activity compared to the starting radioactivity, both decay-corrected to the same point in time

|radionuclides
|unstable forms of chemical element that releases radiation

|rads
|unit of measurement for absorbed dose of ionizing radiation, corresponding to the absorbption of 0.01 Joule/kg

|reactive nonmetals
|elements that are very eager to gain electrons to form chemical bonds

|roentgens
|legacy (inactive) unit of measurement for x-rays and gamma rays

|scalar
|having only a quantity of magnitude (but no direction)

|scm
|standard cubic meter, a unit of volume for a gas measured under standard conditions of temperature and pressure

|Sf
|spontaneous fission

|Sinex SO~2~ monitor
|device used to measure the concentration of sulfur dioxide in the air

|solvent
|a substance used to dissolve other substances

|spallation reaction
|nuclear reaction where a heavy nucleus, struck by a high-energy particle, breaks apart and ejects many fragments, including neutrons, protons, and lighter nuclei

|substrate
|base layer or material on which a nuclear target is prepared or deposited

|tensor
|a mathematical object that can describe the properties of a nucleus, a way to describe physical quantities that are more complex than a single number

|tetraphenyl arsonium chloride
|organoarsenic compound used as a reagent in organic synthesis and analytical chemistry

|tracer
|substances added into a system which can be used to track movement and behavior of physical, chemical, or biological processes

|transition metals
|harder metals which are good conductors and can more easily form colored, chemical compounds

|transuranic
|has a higher atomic number than uranium (92)

|tropopause
|the boundary layer between the troposphere and the stratosphere, located at an altitude of roughly 8 km at the poles and 18 km at the equator

|upwind
|somethingor somewhere positioned against the direction of the wind

|velocity
|the rate at which an object's position changes and includes both magnitude (speed) and direction

|weather vane (vane)
|a device used to show the direction of the wind

|x-ray fluorescence (XRF)
|analytical technique that identifies the elemental composition of a material

|===
